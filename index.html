Most perfume is made to be noticed. Scentsburg is made to be remembered — a low register of smoke, resin and warm skin, worn close enough that only the people you let close will know it.

The Composition

How it unfolds, hour by hour.

i.First 15 minutes

Top

  • Calabrian Bergamot
    Bright and green, pressed rather than distilled, so it opens a little bitter.
  • Pink Peppercorn
    A dry, faintly smoked spark that keeps the citrus from turning sweet.
  • Cardamom
    One green thread running underneath, more felt than smelled.
ii.1 – 4 hours

Heart

  • Smoked Cistus Labdanum
    The heart of the amber — resinous, leathered, faintly like a snuffed candle.
  • Dried Rose Absolute
    Rose with the water taken out of it — closer to tea than to flowers.
  • Fig Leaf Accord
    Green and slightly milky, cut into the resin like shade into heat.
iii.4+ hours, on skin

Base

  • Papua New Guinea Sandalwood
    Warm and slightly milky, the note that stays longest on a scarf or sleeve.
  • Ambergris Accord
    Salt-skin and warmth, built without any animal material, worn very close.
  • Smoked Vetiver
    Root and ash, the last thing to leave — often still there the next morning.
Eau de Parfum

Poured in batches of two hundred, never more, so the amber never has to be stretched to keep up with demand.

Vesper No. I is blended by hand in a single workshop and left to rest for six weeks before it is bottled. Each flacon is numbered against that batch, and the batch is retired once it sells out — there is no reorder, only the next one, which will smell like itself and not like a repeat.

The House

Built by one nose, not a committee.

Scentsburg was founded on the idea that a perfume house could stay the size of a single workshop and still be taken seriously — one perfumer, one supplier for each raw material, and no scent released before it has been worn, in the real world, for a full year.

There is no line to complete, no seasonal collection to fill. Each Scentsburg number exists because a particular hour of a particular day demanded it, and the house has made exactly three since it opened.

I wanted a smoke you could wear to a wedding — never quite comfortable, never quite alarming. — Founder & Perfumer, Scentsburg
Wearing It

Worn low, not sprayed high.

Inner Wrist

One press, held a foot away from the skin rather than against it — amber blooms slowly and doesn't need to be helped along.

Base of the Throat

Warmth rises. A scent worn here reaches a room before a handshake does, which is exactly as much projection as amber needs.

Inside the Elbow

A quieter point, mostly for the wearer — the last place the smoke is still noticeable to you, hours after everyone else has stopped.
